H2: What Is Included in Interior Painting
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Every interior painting project includes surface preparation, priming where needed, two coats of finish paint on walls, separate paint on trim and doors, furniture protection, and thorough cleanup before we leave each day and at project completion.

Surface preparation is the first and most important step. We clean wall surfaces, lightly sand where needed, fill nail holes and minor dings with appropriate filler, and caulk gaps along trim and at ceiling lines. For older Connecticut homes with plaster walls, prep may include skim coating sections where the plaster has cracked, separated from the lath, or been previously repaired poorly. We do not paint over problems. If a surface is not ready to hold paint properly, we address it before we prime.

Priming is applied wherever it is needed: over repaired areas, on surfaces with staining or bleed-through risk, on bare wood, and on any surface where adhesion may be a concern. We do not skip priming to save time.

We apply two coats of finish paint on all wall surfaces. Trim, doors, and moldings are painted separately with an appropriate sheen for durability and a clean line. Kitchens and bathrooms receive moisture-resistant formulations. High-traffic areas get durable satin or semi-gloss finishes. Bedrooms and low-traffic spaces typically receive a flat or matte finish that reads well in natural and artificial light.

Furniture is moved away from walls and protected with drop cloths before we begin. Floors are covered. Fixtures and hardware are masked. We clean up daily and fully at project end. The final walkthrough is our chance to catch anything that needs touching up before we call the job complete.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Interior painting in Connecticut costs $3 to $6 per square foot of wall area, covering prep, primer, two finish coats on walls, and trim painting. Rooms with high ceilings, significant prep needs, or extensive trim work trend toward the higher end of the range.

Additional prep work beyond standard surface repair is priced separately if discovered during the walk-through. We identify anything that needs attention before we quote the project so the estimate reflects what the job actually requires.

H2: When to Consider Interior Painting
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Interior painting makes sense as a standalone project when your walls show visible wear, scuffing, or dated colors, but the underlying surfaces are in good condition and no structural work is needed. It is one of the most cost-effective ways to refresh the feel of a home without a full renovation.

If you are planning a kitchen remodel, bathroom update, or basement finishing project, painting the adjacent rooms at the same time gives you a cohesive result and avoids a second round of setup and disruption later. When painting follows carpentry work on new trim or built-ins, the sequence matters, and we schedule it correctly within the broader project.

Painting is also well-timed when you have recently moved into a home and want to put your mark on it before furniture is settled in. With rooms emptier than they will be later, it is the most efficient moment for the work.

One note specific to Connecticut: homes built before 1978 may have lead-based paint on original surfaces. This applies to a significant portion of the housing stock across our service area, including Greenwich, New Haven, Hamden, and many other towns where pre-war and mid-century construction is common. Before any sanding, scraping, or surface disturbance on these homes, lead testing is appropriate and required under EPA RRP rules for contractors. We follow proper lead-safe work practices when applicable, and we can walk you through what that means for your specific project.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
H2: How Interior Painting Works With Other Services
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Interior painting coordinates naturally with nearly every renovation service we provide. In kitchen remodeling and bathroom remodeling projects, new wall surfaces and freshly installed trim need finish paint as part of the completed scope. We include painting at the right point in the construction sequence so new tile grout, caulk, and trim are fully cured before paint is applied.

Interior carpentry work, whether that is new crown molding, wainscoting, or custom built-ins, always finishes with paint or stain. When we handle both the carpentry and the painting, we schedule them in the correct order and do not leave the coordination to you.

Basement finishing projects include painting as part of the complete scope. New drywall requires primer and finish paint, and we handle it within the same project timeline rather than as a separate engagement later.

H2: Interior Painting Questions
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

H3: How much does interior painting cost in Connecticut?

Interior painting in Connecticut costs $3 to $6 per square foot of wall area, with a typical single room running $800 to $2,500 and a complete whole-home interior ranging from $8,000 to $25,000 or more. The main variables are the size of the space, ceiling height, the amount of trim and molding that needs to be painted separately, and the condition of the surfaces going in.

Rooms that need significant patching, skim coating on damaged plaster, or lead-safe prep procedures will cost more than rooms in good condition with standard walls. We walk through the space before quoting so the estimate reflects what the job actually requires. There are no line items that appear after the project starts.

H3: Do you prep the surfaces before painting?

Yes. Surface preparation is the first step on every interior painting project we take on. We fill nail holes, repair dings and minor cracks with appropriate filler, sand rough areas, and caulk gaps along trim and at ceiling lines before priming or painting begins. For older Connecticut homes with plaster walls, prep may include skim coating areas where the plaster has deteriorated or been previously patched improperly.

We also follow lead-safe work practices for pre-1978 homes when sanding or scraping is involved. Proper prep is what determines how long a paint job holds up. A coat of paint over unprepared surfaces may look fine on day one and show its problems within a year. We do the prep correctly the first time.

H3: Can you paint occupied rooms or do I need to move out?

You do not need to move out. We work in occupied homes regularly. We move furniture away from walls, cover floors and fixtures, and work one room or one area at a time to keep disruption contained. The space is usable again within a day or two per room once paint has cured.

For whole-home projects, we sequence the work to keep the most-used spaces livable while we move through the house. Kitchens and primary bathrooms are typically scheduled for efficient completion so you are not without them for extended periods. We discuss the sequence during the consultation and plan around your household's routine.
