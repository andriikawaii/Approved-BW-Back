H2: What Is Included in a Flooring Installation
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A complete flooring installation covers subfloor assessment and preparation, removal and disposal of existing flooring, underlayment, the installation itself, transition strips, base shoe or quarter-round molding, and cleanup, covering everything from the floor up to finished edges.

We start every flooring project with a thorough assessment of the existing subfloor. We check for level, moisture content, structural integrity, soft spots, and any damage that needs correction before new material goes down. A quality floor is only as good as what is underneath it, and subfloor problems that get covered up show up quickly in the finished surface as squeaks, movement, gaps, and buckling that no amount of good installation technique can prevent.

Standard installation includes removal and disposal of existing flooring, subfloor preparation and leveling where required, appropriate underlayment for the material being installed, the installation itself to manufacturer specifications, transition strips between rooms and between different flooring types, and quarter-round or base shoe molding where the floor meets the baseboard. We move standard furniture within the work area at the start of installation and replace it once the new floor is in.

Additional scope that adds cost includes subfloor repair or replacement for damaged sections, custom patterns or inlays, floor leveling with self-leveling compound over larger areas, or the removal of multiple layers of existing flooring. We assess and communicate these conditions before the project begins whenever they're visible, and we communicate them immediately if we find them after work starts.

H2: Flooring Cost in Connecticut
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Flooring installation in Connecticut costs $6 to $25 per square foot installed depending on the material, with most projects falling between these ranges based on the specific product and subfloor conditions. The table below covers installed costs (materials, underlayment, and labor) for the most common flooring types we install.

| Flooring Type | Installed Cost | Best Applications |
|---------------|----------------|-------------------|
| LVP (Luxury Vinyl Plank) | $6–$14 per sq ft | Basements, kitchens, high-traffic areas, moisture-prone spaces |
| Solid Hardwood | $12–$25 per sq ft | Living rooms, dining rooms, bedrooms, above-grade only |
| Engineered Hardwood | $8–$18 per sq ft | Basements, areas with seasonal humidity changes |
| Tile | $12–$25 per sq ft | Bathrooms, kitchens, entryways |
| Carpet | $4–$12 per sq ft | Bedrooms, basement recreation rooms |

These ranges reflect installed cost including materials and labor. Subfloor repair, removal of multiple flooring layers, and custom patterns or inlays are priced separately based on the specific conditions we find. We provide a written proposal before any work begins so you know exactly what you're paying for.

A few cost factors specific to Connecticut homes: pre-1980 homes sometimes have original hardwood under carpet that's worth restoring rather than replacing, which can be more cost-effective than new material. Homes with radiant heat require flooring products rated for that application. Basement flooring requires moisture testing before installation, which affects material selection.

H2: Project Timeline
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Most flooring installations in Connecticut complete in two to five days. A single room takes one to two days. Whole-home installations covering 1,000 or more square feet typically take three to five days. Large whole-home projects or those requiring significant subfloor work may extend to seven to ten days.

| Project Scope | Timeline |
|---------------|----------|
| Single room (under 250 sq ft) | 1–2 days |
| Multiple rooms or partial floor (250–600 sq ft) | 2–3 days |
| Whole-floor installation (600–1,200 sq ft) | 3–5 days |
| Whole-home installation or complex subfloor work | 5–10 days |
| Hardwood requiring sand and finish | Add 2–3 days for drying |

Flooring is typically installed near the end of a larger renovation, after drywall, painting, and cabinet installation, so when flooring is part of a kitchen remodel or basement finishing project, the overall project timeline is what governs. We schedule flooring work within the broader project sequence to avoid delays.

Material acclimation is also a scheduling consideration. Solid hardwood requires three to five days on-site before installation begins, allowing the material to adjust to the humidity levels of your home. Engineered hardwood and LVP have shorter or no acclimation requirements. We factor this into the project schedule so acclimation happens before the installation date, not as a delay once the crew arrives.

H2: Our Flooring Installation Process
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Every flooring project follows a five-step process, beginning at the consultation with a subfloor assessment and material review, and ending with a final walkthrough once installation is complete.

### Step 1: Consultation
We visit your home or connect via Google Meet or Zoom to discuss your goals, assess the space, and answer your questions. No charge. No obligation. For flooring projects, the on-site visit lets us assess existing subfloor conditions, check for moisture in below-grade spaces, identify transition points between rooms and flooring types, and get an accurate square footage count. Remote consultations work well for simpler projects where you can share photos and measurements.

### Step 2: Planning
You receive a clear written proposal covering exactly what's included, how long it will take, and what it costs. Line items are specific. No surprises mid-project. The proposal includes removal and disposal, subfloor preparation, materials, labor, transitions, and molding. If we identify subfloor conditions during the consultation that may require additional work, we include a clear explanation of what we found and how we'll handle it.

### Step 3: Selections
We guide you through material choices (flooring types, finishes, and specific products) with options at different price points. We communicate lead times clearly so selections are made on schedule before installation begins. Connecticut's climate makes material selection more than a purely aesthetic decision, and we walk you through which options are appropriate for each room and level of your home. We won't recommend solid hardwood for a basement or a product that won't hold up to the humidity conditions in your specific location.

### Step 4: Build
Construction begins on the agreed schedule. You receive daily updates on progress and a clean job site at the end of every workday. Our crews arrive when scheduled. For flooring projects, this means the subfloor work is done first, the acclimation period is respected, and the installation proceeds systematically from one area to the next. If we encounter a subfloor condition we couldn't see at consultation (a soft spot under carpet, a moisture reading that is higher than expected), we stop, communicate, and present options before proceeding.

### Step 5: Walkthrough
We walk through the finished project together. If anything needs attention, we address it before calling the project complete. For flooring, the walkthrough covers every room, every transition strip, every edge, every transition between flooring types, and the condition of the base shoe or quarter-round molding. We want you to be fully satisfied with the finished result before we pack up.

H2: Why Connecticut Flooring Installation Requires Local Expertise
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Connecticut's climate creates specific challenges for flooring that a contractor without local experience may not account for. Seasonal humidity swings, from the humid summers to the dry heating season in winter, cause solid hardwood to expand and contract in ways that produce gaps, squeaks, and cupping when the material is not properly acclimated or when the wrong product is used in the wrong location.

Proper acclimation before installation is essential. Solid hardwood needs three to five days on-site in your home's environment before it can be installed. That time lets the wood reach equilibrium with the ambient humidity so the boards are not expanding or contracting immediately after installation. Skipping acclimation, or delivering material to a job site and installing it the same day, is one of the most common flooring mistakes we see undone during renovation work. We always allow the full acclimation period before installation begins.

Coastal homes in towns like Westport, Guilford, Milford, Madison, and Branford have elevated ambient humidity year-round from proximity to Long Island Sound. Salt air and higher baseline moisture levels affect material selection in these areas. Solid hardwood in a coastal home that experiences regular seasonal humidity above 60 percent will move more than the same product installed inland. We assess the specific conditions at your home and recommend accordingly.

Basement flooring has its own requirements in Connecticut. We test moisture levels in concrete slabs before recommending any flooring product for below-grade installation. LVP and tile are the reliable choices below grade. Engineered hardwood can work when moisture levels are confirmed to be consistently within acceptable ranges. Solid hardwood and carpet do not belong in Connecticut basements. This is not a product preference; it's a function of what Connecticut geology and groundwater conditions do to materials that are not designed for moisture exposure.

Pre-1980 Connecticut homes are also worth examining before assuming replacement is the right choice. A significant number of homes from this era have original hardwood flooring under carpet that was covered during a renovation decades ago. That wood is often structurally sound and worth restoring through sanding and refinishing rather than replacing. We assess what's there before recommending what to do with it.

H2: Flooring Questions
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

### What is the best flooring for Connecticut homes?

The best flooring depends on the room, the level of the home, and the specific moisture conditions at your property. For above-grade living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms, solid hardwood is a proven choice that holds up to Connecticut's climate when properly acclimated and finished; oak, maple, and hickory are particularly durable species. For kitchens, entryways, and high-traffic areas above grade, LVP and tile both work well and handle regular moisture exposure from cooking, foot traffic, and weather tracked in from outside. For basements and below-grade spaces, LVP is the most reliable choice in Connecticut: it's 100 percent waterproof, stable through temperature and humidity swings, and comfortable underfoot. For bedrooms, carpet remains popular and appropriate above grade where moisture exposure is not a factor. Coastal homes near Long Island Sound may need to avoid solid hardwood in areas of elevated ambient humidity even above grade, and we assess those conditions at the consultation.

### How long does flooring installation take?

Most residential flooring projects complete in two to five days. A single room (a bedroom, a home office, a dining room) takes one to two days including subfloor preparation, installation, and trim work. Multi-room installations covering 250 to 600 square feet typically take two to three days. Full-floor or whole-home installations covering 600 to 1,200 square feet take three to five days. Large whole-home projects or those with significant subfloor repairs may extend to seven to ten days. If you're installing solid hardwood, add three to five days for the required acclimation period before installation begins. When flooring is part of a larger renovation (a kitchen remodel, a basement finishing project), the flooring timeline is embedded in the overall project schedule and does not typically extend the overall duration.

### Can you install new flooring over existing flooring?

In some cases, yes. LVP and engineered hardwood can often be installed directly over existing hard surfaces (tile, vinyl, or hardwood) provided the subfloor is level, structurally sound, and within acceptable moisture parameters. We assess each situation individually. The key factors are whether the existing surface is level enough to avoid telegraphing irregularities through the new floor, whether the added height at thresholds and doors creates problems, and whether the existing material is adhered securely. In cases where overlay installation is appropriate, it saves the cost and time of removal. In cases where it is not (damaged subfloor, excessive height buildup, moisture concerns, or a material that has failed), removal is the right choice and we'll tell you why. We don't cover up problems.

### What is the difference between solid and engineered hardwood?

Solid hardwood is milled from a single piece of wood, typically three-quarters of an inch thick, and can be sanded and refinished multiple times over its lifetime. It performs best in above-grade rooms with stable humidity: living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms in Connecticut homes that maintain consistent indoor humidity year-round. Engineered hardwood is constructed with a real wood veneer layer bonded over a plywood core. The cross-layer construction makes it significantly more resistant to the expansion and contraction that humidity changes cause in solid wood. Engineered hardwood is appropriate for below-grade spaces, rooms over concrete slabs, and Connecticut homes where seasonal humidity swings are pronounced. It can typically be refinished once or twice depending on the veneer thickness, compared to several times for solid hardwood. The right choice depends on where in your home the flooring is going and what the moisture conditions are. We'll tell you which one makes sense for your specific situation.

### Do I need to move furniture before flooring installation?

We move standard furniture within the work area as part of the installation. This includes sofas, chairs, tables, beds, dressers, and similar household furniture. Heavy or specialty items such as grand pianos, gun safes, large antique armoires, or other exceptionally heavy pieces require specialty movers that we can help you coordinate in advance. Disconnecting and reconnecting appliances in the kitchen is part of the scope when flooring extends into kitchen areas. Anything mounted to walls, like bookcases that are anchored or built-in units, should be discussed at the consultation so we can plan accordingly. We'll let you know at the proposal stage exactly what we'll handle and if there's anything that needs separate coordination.
