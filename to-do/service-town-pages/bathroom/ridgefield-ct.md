H2: What Is Included in a Ridgefield Bathroom Remodel

A complete bathroom remodel in Ridgefield covers demolition, waterproofing, tile, plumbing fixtures, vanity, electrical, ventilation, and all required permits, managed as a coordinated project from the initial assessment through the final walkthrough.

We start with full demolition of existing tile, fixtures, and flooring. Before any new materials go in, we inspect the subfloor, wall framing, and existing plumbing and electrical lines. Ridgefield's older colonial and cape housing stock regularly presents conditions that need to be addressed at that stage: original cast iron supply and drain lines that are corroded or undersized, galvanized supply lines that have degraded to a point where water pressure and quality are affected, subfloor framing that isn't adequate for the weight of modern tile systems, and 100-amp electrical panels that may not support heated floors or steam units without an upgrade.

We identify and communicate those conditions before we proceed. If a plumbing supply line needs to be replaced, you'll hear about it with an explanation of what we found and what your options are. It will not appear as a surprise invoice at the end of the project. That standard applies to every condition we uncover during demo.

Waterproofing is installed behind all shower and tub tile using membrane systems, not adhesive over drywall. We use cement board in wet areas as standard practice on every project. We've encountered bathrooms in older Ridgefield homes where tile was applied directly over plaster or standard drywall with no waterproofing behind it. Those bathrooms look fine until they don't, and when they fail, the damage extends well beyond the tile. We don't take shortcuts on waterproofing.

Exhaust fans are properly sized for the room volume and ducted to the exterior. In older Ridgefield homes, it's not uncommon to find fans that were vented into an attic crawl space. That creates mold conditions over time and in cold winters contributes to ice dam problems. We correct that when we find it and include proper exterior venting in every bathroom we renovate.

New work includes tile on floors and walls, vanity and countertop installation, all plumbing fixture connections, electrical updates for lighting and GFCI outlets, and exhaust fan installation or replacement. Every project includes permit coordination with the Ridgefield Building Department, daily cleanup, and dust barriers protecting the rest of the house throughout construction.

Pre-1978 homes are assessed for lead paint before we disturb plaster or drywall. Pre-1980 homes may also have asbestos floor tile, which requires testing before demolition proceeds. We handle both assessments correctly and communicate the results and their implications before work begins.

H2: Bathroom Remodeling Questions for Ridgefield, CT

### Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Ridgefield, CT?

Yes. The Ridgefield Building Department issues separate building, electrical, and plumbing permits for bathroom remodeling work, each with its own inspection process. Walk-in shower installations require a building permit. New lighting circuits, GFCI outlets, and dedicated circuits for heated floors require an electrical permit. Any plumbing changes, including supply line replacement or drain relocation, require a plumbing permit. We handle all three applications and all associated inspections as a standard part of every project.

### How long does a bathroom remodel take in Ridgefield?

Most full bathroom renovations in Ridgefield complete in three to six weeks of active construction. Basic refreshes without structural or plumbing changes typically run two to three weeks. Full gut renovations with custom tile, a new shower enclosure, and vanity installation run four to six weeks. Projects involving layout changes, plumbing relocation, or structural work extend beyond that. Your written proposal includes a specific project schedule with your expected start and completion dates.

### What conditions should I expect when gutting an older Ridgefield bathroom?

In Ridgefield's older colonial and cape housing stock, demolition frequently uncovers corroded galvanized supply lines, cast iron drain pipes that are cracked or scaled, subfloor framing that's undersized for a heavy tile system, and electrical panels running at 100 amps that can't support heated floors or steam units without an upgrade. Pre-1978 homes require lead paint assessment, and pre-1980 homes may have asbestos floor tile that requires testing before demo proceeds. None of these are unusual. They're normal conditions in this housing stock. We identify them during our initial assessment, price them clearly in the written proposal, and walk you through what we found before any work begins.

### What's the difference between a basic refresh and a full gut renovation?

A refresh updates surfaces and fixtures without opening walls or changing the layout: new vanity, updated fixtures, new flooring, paint, and sometimes new lighting. A full gut involves removing everything to the studs, which is the only way to address waterproofing behind tile, correct subfloor issues, relocate plumbing, or change the layout. Refreshes in Ridgefield start around $15,000. Full gut renovations start around $28,000 and increase based on scope and material selections. For older homes where the original waterproofing was inadequate or the plumbing needs attention, a full gut is often the right answer even when the surfaces look fine from the outside.

### Do you handle lead paint and asbestos testing before demolition?

Yes. Pre-1978 homes require lead paint assessment before we disturb any plaster or drywall, and pre-1980 homes may have asbestos-containing floor tile that requires testing before demolition begins. We manage both assessment processes, communicate the results and what they mean for the project, and handle proper containment and disposal where required. These steps add a small amount of time to the front end of the project, but they're required by regulation and they protect your family and our crew. We build them into the project schedule so they don't come as a surprise.
