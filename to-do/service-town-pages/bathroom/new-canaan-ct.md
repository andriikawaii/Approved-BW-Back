Bathroom remodeling in New Canaan runs from approximately $20,000 for a basic fixture and vanity refresh to $150,000 or more for a project involving layout changes, structural modifications, or added square footage in a primary bath.

The table below reflects current project ranges for New Canaan homes. Two market-specific factors affect pricing in this town. First, design expectations here are higher than in most Connecticut markets. Walk-in showers replacing tub and shower combinations are extremely common: clients have often already done one bathroom renovation and know what they want. We assess panel capacity during planning and include the upgrade in the written scope when it applies.

H2: What Is Included in a New Canaan Bathroom Remodel
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A full bathroom remodel in New Canaan covers demolition, waterproofing, tile, plumbing fixtures, vanity, electrical, ventilation, and all required permits, managed as a single coordinated project from start to final walkthrough.

We begin with complete demolition of existing tile, fixtures, vanity, and flooring down to the subfloor and wall framing. Before any new materials are installed, we assess the subfloor condition, the plumbing line configuration, and the electrical capacity. New Canaan homes built between the 1940s and 1970s, including MCM properties and traditional colonials, can present conditions that need to be addressed before finish work begins: undersized electrical service, aging galvanized drain lines, subfloor framing not adequate for heavy tile, or plumbing venting that does not meet current code. We document what we find, explain what it means for the project, and present options before proceeding.

In mid-century modern homes, the assessment extends to design intent. MCM bathrooms were typically designed as integrated components of a larger open interior plan. Floating vanities, minimal hardware, and material selections that connected to the palette of the rest of the home were common and deliberate. Replacing original fixtures with a standard vanity and builder-grade tile can work against the architecture in ways that are difficult to reverse. We approach MCM renovations with the original design intent in mind: appropriate vanity profiles, material continuity with the rest of the home's interior, and sensitivity to the relationship between the bathroom and adjacent spaces. A renovation that respects the architecture and one that ignores it are not the same thing, and they do not cost the same to correct later.

Waterproofing is installed behind all shower and wet-wall tile using a membrane system applied over cement board. This is standard practice on every project, not an upgrade. Tile work covers floor and wall installations with consistent grout joints and proper setting materials for the substrate. Vanity and countertop installation, all plumbing fixture connections, electrical work for new lighting and GFCI outlets, and exhaust fan installation are all included. Exhaust fans are sized correctly and ducted to the exterior, not into wall cavities or attic spaces, which is a condition we find and correct in older homes.

If the project involves exterior modifications, a new window, a change to the wall envelope, and the property falls within the New Canaan Historic District or the Silvermine area, a Historic District Commission Certificate of Appropriateness is required before a building permit will be issued. We identify that requirement during the planning phase and manage the HDC application as part of the project. New Canaan also enforces strict zoning setbacks and floor area ratio limits. Any project involving added square footage requires a zoning compliance review before scope is finalized.

Every project includes all permit applications and inspection coordination, dust barriers throughout construction, daily cleanup, and a final walkthrough.
