Bathroom remodeling in Orange typically ranges from $14,000 for a basic refresh to $100,000 or more for a project with significant layout changes, with most full gut renovations landing between $25,000 and $50,000.

The table below reflects typical 2025 pricing for Orange bathroom projects. The scope of work, materials selected, and whether any structural changes are involved are the primary factors that determine where a project lands in these ranges.

| Tier | Scope | Range |

Second, adding heated floors or a steam shower typically increases the electrical load enough that a 100-amp panel will require an upgrade. We identify both of these conditions at the consultation and build them into the written scope when they apply. H2: What Is Included in a Bathroom Remodel in Orange
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A complete bathroom remodel in Orange covers every element from demolition through final walkthrough: waterproofing, tile, plumbing fixtures, vanity, electrical, ventilation, and all permits coordinated as a single project.

We begin with full demolition of existing tile, fixtures, vanity, and flooring. Before any new material goes in, we inspect the subfloor, wall framing, and plumbing lines. Orange homes from the 1950s through the 1980s regularly turn up conditions behind the walls that are worth addressing while the space is open. Corroded drain lines, undersized supply piping, or subfloor damage beneath old vinyl flooring are not unusual. We assess those conditions and report back with options before finishing work begins.

Waterproofing goes in behind all shower and tub tile using membrane systems. We use cement board in wet areas as a standard practice, not as an upgrade. This is the correct approach for Connecticut's climate, where seasonal temperature swings and winter heating create conditions that can cause poorly waterproofed bathrooms to fail within a few years.

New work includes tile on floors and walls, vanity and countertop installation, all plumbing fixture connections, electrical updates for lighting and GFCI outlets, and exhaust fan installation or replacement. Exhaust fans must be sized correctly and ducted to the exterior. When we find a fan vented into an attic, we correct it. That condition causes moisture damage and, in winter, ice dams.

Every project includes permit coordination with the Orange Building Department, daily cleanup, dust barriers to protect adjacent rooms, and a final walkthrough where we review every detail together.

H2: Bathroom Conditions Specific to Orange Homes
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Orange's residential character is defined by split-level and colonial homes built from the 1950s through the 1980s, and bathroom renovation in those homes comes with conditions that a contractor working in town needs to understand before the first nail comes out.

Split-level bathrooms are typically small by modern standards. The footprint was designed for the household expectations of the 1960s, and there is often limited flexibility for expansion without getting into structural work. Opening walls in a split-level to widen a bathroom or reconfigure the layout requires assessing what is load-bearing in a building type that distributes loads differently than a conventional two-story colonial. We assess the structural picture at the consultation.

Galvanized supply lines are common throughout Orange's older housing stock. Galvanized pipe corrodes internally over time, reducing water pressure and eventually failing. When walls are already open for a bathroom renovation, evaluating and replacing supply lines is straightforward and makes economic sense. We identify what is there during the scope review and give you an honest assessment of whether replacement is warranted.

Electrical panels in Orange homes from the 1960s and 1970s are frequently 100-amp service. That capacity was adequate for the time but is often not sufficient for contemporary bathroom features. Heated floor systems and steam generators draw meaningful current loads. When those features are part of a project, we evaluate the panel at the consultation and include an upgrade in the scope when it is needed, rather than discovering the issue mid-project.

There is no historic district in Orange, and the Orange Building Department has a straightforward permitting process. We know the department well. It is a five-minute drive from our office, and we handle all permit applications and inspection scheduling as a standard part of every project.

We'll discuss your goals, assess the space, and answer questions. In-person or video call, your choice. For Orange projects, in-person visits from our office require no meaningful travel time. ### Step 2: Planning
You'll receive a clear proposal with exactly what's included, how long it will take, and what it costs. No surprises. Tile work, plumbing, vanity installation, electrical, waterproofing, and permits are broken out as separate line items so you can see exactly where the budget is going. ### Step 3: Selections
We guide you through material choices: tile, vanity, countertop, fixtures, shower enclosure, and lighting, with options at different price points. We communicate lead times clearly so selections are finalized before construction begins. If a tile or vanity has a significant lead time, we identify that early and order it promptly so it does not delay your start date.

### Step 4: Build
Construction begins on the agreed schedule. You'll get daily updates on progress, a clean job site at the end of every workday, and crews who arrive when scheduled. If demolition uncovers a condition behind the walls that changes the scope, we contact you that day with an explanation and your options before proceeding. In Orange's 1960s and 1970s housing stock, that does happen, and we handle it the same way every time.

### Step 5: Walkthrough
We walk through the finished project together. We check every grout line, every fixture connection, every light, every cabinet hinge. If anything needs attention, we address it before calling the project complete. Your acceptance at the final walkthrough is the last step.

H2: Bathroom Remodeling Questions: Orange, CT
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

### Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Orange, CT?

Yes. The Orange Building Department requires permits for any bathroom renovation that involves plumbing, electrical, or structural work. Replacing a tub with a walk-in shower typically requires a building permit; electrical work including new lighting circuits and GFCI outlets requires an electrical permit. Most full bathroom renovations require both a building and an electrical permit, and some projects also require a plumbing permit. We handle all permit applications and inspection scheduling as part of the project. The Orange Building Department is a five-minute drive from our office, and we know the process well. You do not need to manage it separately.

### How much does a bathroom remodel cost in Orange, CT?

Bathroom remodeling in Orange typically costs $14,000 to $25,000 for a basic refresh with new vanity, fixtures, flooring, and paint; $25,000 to $50,000 for a full gut renovation with new tile, a new shower or tub, vanity, and lighting; and $50,000 to $75,000 for a high-end project with a walk-in shower, custom tile, and heated floors. Projects involving layout changes or structural work run $75,000 to $100,000 or more. Two line items specific to Orange's housing stock that are worth knowing upfront: galvanized supply line replacement and electrical panel upgrades are not unusual in homes from the 1960s and 1970s, and both belong in the written scope before construction starts, not as additions after.

### Can I use my bathroom during the remodel?

Not the bathroom being renovated. We seal the work area with dust barriers for safety and dust control, and the space is not functional until the project is complete. If your home has a second bathroom, you will have access to it throughout the project. If the bathroom being renovated is your only bathroom, we discuss that directly during the planning phase and work through a realistic approach before construction begins, whether that means accelerating certain portions of the work to restore basic function as quickly as possible or making other arrangements. You will know exactly what access looks like before we start.

### What is the difference between a bathroom refresh and a full remodel?

A bathroom refresh updates surfaces and fixtures without opening walls or changing the plumbing layout. It typically includes a new vanity and countertop, updated faucets and hardware, new flooring, paint, and sometimes a lighting update. The supply lines and drain connections stay in place. A full remodel guts the space to the studs and starts over, which allows for proper waterproofing behind tile, subfloor repairs, plumbing relocation, and any layout reconfiguration. Refreshes in Orange typically start around $14,000. Full gut renovations start around $25,000 and go up based on scope and materials. The right choice depends on the current condition of the bathroom and what you are trying to accomplish. We make that assessment at the consultation and give you a direct recommendation.

### Can you replace a tub with a walk-in shower in an Orange split-level bathroom?

Yes, and it is one of the more common requests we handle in Orange. The considerations specific to split-level bathrooms are the footprint size and the drain location. A new walk-in shower drain needs to be positioned with proper slope for barrier-free entry, and in a small split-level bathroom, that can require some floor framing adjustment. We assess what the existing drain location allows during the consultation and confirm the structural conditions before proposing a layout. Walk-in shower installations require a building permit in Orange, which we handle as part of the project. If the bathroom currently has only one tub and you are replacing it with a shower, we'll discuss the resale implications during the consultation as well.
