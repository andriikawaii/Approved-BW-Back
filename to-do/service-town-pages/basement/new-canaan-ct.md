## What Is Included

A complete basement finishing project covers moisture assessment, framing, electrical, insulation, drywall, ceiling, flooring, paint, trim, and all required permits and inspections: from bare concrete to a finished, permitted space ready to use.

We start every New Canaan project with a thorough inspection of the foundation walls, floor slab, and existing drainage. We look for active seepage, efflorescence, floor cracks, and conditions that indicate ongoing moisture movement through the foundation. If mitigation is required, we address it before any framing proposal is written. That sequence is not negotiable.

Once the foundation is cleared, we frame walls to the approved layout, run electrical circuits for outlets, lighting, and dedicated loads, and insulate exterior walls with rigid foam appropriate for below-grade conditions. Drywall, taping, and ceiling work follow. Ceiling options include drywall for a clean permanent finish or a suspended ceiling where access to plumbing and mechanicals above needs to remain available.

Flooring, paint, trim, and doors complete the project. Common additions within the same scope include egress window installation, full bathroom construction, wet bar plumbing, home office built-ins, and custom shelving. We handle all permit filings and coordinate every required inspection with the New Canaan building department.

## Basement Finishing Questions for New Canaan, CT


### Do I need a permit to finish a basement in New Canaan, CT?

Yes. Basement finishing requires a building permit in New Canaan, as it does in every Connecticut town. The work involves framing, electrical wiring, and often plumbing, all of which require licensed work and building department inspections. Some projects require separate permits for electrical and plumbing work in addition to the main building permit. If your project includes an egress window on a street-facing elevation, it may also require historic commission review. We handle all permit filings and coordinate every required inspection. You do not manage any part of the permitting process.


### How does BuiltWell handle moisture in Connecticut basements?

We assess every basement before proposing any finishing work. During the consultation, we inspect foundation walls for seepage, efflorescence, and staining; check the floor slab for cracks and moisture; evaluate exterior grading around the foundation; and measure humidity levels. If conditions require correction, we address them first, with sump pump installation, interior perimeter drainage, foundation crack injection, or exterior grading corrections. Moisture mitigation is completed and confirmed stable before framing begins. In New Canaan, where both poured concrete and older foundations appear across different eras of construction, the assessment findings drive the proposal.


### Does a finished basement require an egress window?

If the finished basement includes a bedroom, Connecticut building code requires at least one egress window in that room, meeting minimum dimensions for opening width, height, net clear opening area, and sill height. Recreation rooms, home offices, and workout areas do not require egress windows by code, but they add natural light, improve ventilation, and improve the finished space. In New Canaan, egress windows on street-facing elevations may require historic commission review. We identify that requirement during the planning phase, not after permit submission.


### What is the best flooring for a finished basement in CT?

Luxury vinyl plank is the most practical choice for most finished basements in Connecticut: fully waterproof, stable in temperature-variable below-grade environments, comfortable underfoot, and $6 to $14 per square foot installed. Engineered hardwood is an option in New Canaan basements where moisture levels are confirmed to be consistently within acceptable ranges, the cross-layer construction handles humidity better than solid hardwood. Tile is appropriate for bathroom and wet bar areas. Solid hardwood and carpet are not suitable for below-grade spaces in Connecticut's climate. We test moisture levels before making flooring recommendations and present options that will hold up in your specific space.


### Can BuiltWell add a bathroom to my finished basement?

Yes. We install full bathrooms in finished basements as part of the overall project. If no plumbing rough-in exists below the slab, we break the concrete floor to install drain lines, tie them into the existing drain stack, and pour new concrete once rough plumbing is inspected and approved. This adds $8,000 to $15,000 depending on distance to the existing stack, drain routing complexity, and fixture selections. The bathroom rough-in inspection happens early in the construction sequence, before walls close. Planning the bathroom from the start of the project is important, adding one after framing and drywall are complete costs more and takes longer.
