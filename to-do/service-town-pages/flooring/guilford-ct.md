H2: What Is Included in a Guilford Flooring Installation

A complete flooring installation in Guilford covers subfloor assessment and preparation, removal and disposal of existing flooring, appropriate underlayment, installation to manufacturer specifications, transition strips between rooms and flooring types, and base shoe or quarter-round molding where the floor meets the baseboard.

We begin every project with a thorough subfloor assessment. In Guilford's older homes, that assessment sometimes changes what happens next. Original wide-plank floors from the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries are thinner than modern dimensional lumber, which means drum sanding must be done with great care. A modern drum sander can remove more material in one pass than these boards can afford to lose. When we encounter original floors worth preserving, we use hand-scraping or drum methods calibrated to the specific board thickness. We do not run standard equipment over antique flooring and hope for the best.

For homeowners in Guilford's coastal zones, proximity to Long Island Sound creates elevated year-round humidity. Solid hardwood in these conditions requires a minimum of five to seven days of on-site acclimation before installation, which is longer than the standard three to five days recommended in inland homes. Engineered hardwood is the more reliable long-term specification for coastal Guilford properties, and we communicate that recommendation clearly rather than just installing whatever the homeowner happened to select first.

Guilford homeowners frequently ask about low-VOC adhesives and sustainably sourced wood products, and we work with these materials regularly. If those specifications matter to you, mention it at the consultation and we will include compliant products in the selections process.

H2: Flooring Questions

**What is the best flooring for a Connecticut home?**

The best flooring depends on the room, the level of the home, and the specific moisture conditions at your property. Solid hardwood works well in above-grade living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms in homes with stable indoor humidity. Luxury vinyl plank is the most reliable choice for basements and below-grade spaces, as it is fully waterproof and stable through Connecticut's humidity swings. Tile performs well in bathrooms, kitchens, and entryways. Engineered hardwood is appropriate for spaces over concrete slabs, below-grade rooms with confirmed dry conditions, and coastal homes where solid wood would move excessively. Carpet remains a practical choice for bedrooms and above-grade rooms where moisture is not a factor.

**How long does flooring installation take?**

Most residential flooring projects in Connecticut complete in two to five days. A single room takes one to two days. Multi-room installations covering 250 to 600 square feet take two to three days. Whole-floor installations covering 600 to 1,200 square feet take three to five days. In Guilford's coastal homes, solid hardwood requires five to seven days of on-site acclimation before installation can begin, which is factored into the project schedule. When flooring is part of a kitchen remodel or basement finishing project, the flooring work is embedded in the overall project timeline.

**Can new flooring be installed over existing flooring?**

In some cases, yes. Luxury vinyl plank and engineered hardwood can often be installed directly over existing hard surfaces provided the subfloor is level, structurally sound, and within acceptable moisture parameters. In Guilford's older homes, we assess carefully whether the existing layers add too much height at thresholds, whether the existing material is adhered securely, and whether the subfloor underneath has moved or shifted over time. When overlay is appropriate, it saves removal cost. When it is not, we explain why and include removal in the proposal.

**What is the difference between solid and engineered hardwood?**

Solid hardwood is milled from a single piece of wood and can be sanded and refinished multiple times over its lifetime. It performs best in above-grade rooms with stable humidity. Engineered hardwood uses a real wood veneer layer over a plywood core, which makes it significantly more resistant to the expansion and contraction that humidity changes cause. For Guilford homes near the Sound, engineered hardwood is often the better specification because the coastal humidity profile exceeds what solid wood handles comfortably over time. We assess the conditions at your home and tell you which option makes sense for each room.

**Do I need to move my furniture before flooring installation?**

We move standard furniture within the work area as part of the installation. This includes sofas, chairs, tables, beds, dressers, and similar household furniture. Exceptionally heavy items such as grand pianos, large safes, and heavy antique armoires require specialty movers that we can help coordinate in advance. Anything mounted to walls or built into the room should be discussed at the consultation. We will tell you at the proposal stage exactly what we will handle and if anything requires separate coordination.
