H2: What Is Included in a Westport Flooring Installation

A complete flooring installation in Westport covers subfloor assessment and preparation, moisture testing in below-grade spaces, removal and disposal of existing flooring, appropriate underlayment, installation to manufacturer specifications, transition strips, and base shoe or quarter-round molding where the floor meets the baseboard.

We begin every project with a thorough subfloor assessment. In Westport's 1950s through 1970s housing stock, we sometimes encounter original vinyl asbestos tile under current flooring. This is a regulated material that requires licensed abatement before new flooring installation can proceed. We identify this condition during the consultation when it is accessible and communicate it clearly so abatement can be scheduled without delaying the installation.

For basement and below-grade spaces, moisture testing is a required first step. In Westport properties in FEMA flood zones near Compo Beach and the Saugatuck River, luxury vinyl plank installed with a glue-down method is the only wood-look flooring appropriate for basement spaces. A floating installation in a flood zone basement introduces risk that glue-down avoids. We specify the correct installation method for the property and explain why.

For homes with radiant heat systems: a common feature in higher-end Westport properties, we specify flooring products rated for use over radiant systems. Engineered hardwood and luxury vinyl plank both have products rated for radiant heat applications. Solid hardwood is generally not recommended over in-floor radiant heat due to the dimensional movement that repeated heating and cooling cycles cause.

H2: Flooring Questions

**What is the best flooring for a Connecticut home?**

The best flooring depends on the room, the level of the home, and the specific moisture conditions at your property. In coastal Westport, engineered hardwood outperforms solid hardwood in areas with elevated ambient humidity because it resists dimensional movement more effectively. Luxury vinyl plank is the most reliable choice for basements, particularly in flood zone properties, where full waterproofing is essential. Tile performs well in bathrooms, kitchens, and entryways. Solid hardwood is appropriate in above-grade living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms where humidity is stable. Carpet remains a practical option for bedrooms and above-grade spaces where moisture is not a concern.

**How long does flooring installation take?**

Most residential flooring projects in Connecticut complete in two to five days. A single room takes one to two days. Multi-room installations covering 250 to 600 square feet take two to three days. Whole-floor or whole-home installations covering 600 to 1,200 square feet take three to five days. If you are installing solid hardwood, add three to five days for required on-site acclimation before installation can begin. When flooring is part of a kitchen or bathroom remodel in Westport, the flooring installation is sequenced within the overall project timeline and does not typically extend the overall duration.

**Can new flooring be installed over existing flooring?**

In some cases, yes. Luxury vinyl plank and engineered hardwood can often be installed over existing hard surfaces provided the subfloor is level, structurally sound, and within acceptable moisture parameters. In Westport homes from the 1950s through 1970s, existing flooring may contain vinyl asbestos tile that requires licensed abatement before any overlay or replacement can proceed. We assess each situation individually, identify regulated materials where accessible, and specify whether overlay or removal is the appropriate path. We include a clear explanation in the proposal.

**What is the difference between solid and engineered hardwood?**

Solid hardwood is milled from a single piece of wood and can be sanded and refinished multiple times over its lifetime. It performs best in above-grade rooms with stable humidity. Engineered hardwood uses a real wood veneer layer over a plywood core, which makes it significantly more resistant to the expansion and contraction caused by humidity changes. In coastal Westport, where ambient humidity is elevated year-round near the water, engineered hardwood is the more appropriate choice for hardwood flooring installations. For homes with radiant heat, engineered hardwood and LVP rated for radiant applications are the correct products. We will specify which option makes sense for your specific rooms and conditions.

**Do I need to move my furniture before flooring installation?**

We move standard furniture within the work area as part of the installation. This includes sofas, chairs, tables, beds, dressers, and similar household furniture. Exceptionally heavy items such as grand pianos, large safes, or heavy specialty pieces require specialty movers that we can help coordinate in advance. When flooring is being installed as part of a kitchen remodel, disconnecting and reconnecting appliances is included in the scope. Anything mounted to walls or built into the room should be discussed at the consultation. We will confirm at the proposal stage exactly what we handle and whether anything requires separate coordination.
