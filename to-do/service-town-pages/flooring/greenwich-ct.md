H2: What Is Included in a Greenwich Flooring Installation

A complete flooring installation in Greenwich covers subfloor assessment and preparation, removal and disposal of existing flooring, appropriate underlayment, installation to manufacturer specifications, transition strips between rooms and flooring types, and base shoe or quarter-round molding where the floor meets the baseboard.

We begin every project with a thorough subfloor assessment. In Greenwich's pre-1940 homes, original hardwood subfloors frequently show seasonal movement, gaps between boards, and areas that have shifted over decades. These conditions require leveling compound and adequate acclimation time before new flooring goes down. Skipping either step results in a finished floor that performs poorly within the first heating or cooling season.

We test moisture levels in basements and below-grade spaces before recommending any product for those areas. Greenwich homes with stone and rubble foundations experience measurable moisture fluctuation in basements, and we factor that into material selection. Luxury vinyl plank and tile are the reliable choices for below-grade spaces in Greenwich. Solid hardwood is not appropriate below grade in any Greenwich basement regardless of how dry the space appears at inspection.

For Greenwich homeowners replacing flooring in rooms with original trim profiles, we match base shoe and transition pieces to the existing millwork as closely as possible. Wide-plank engineered hardwood at five inches or wider has become a common selection in Greenwich homes where homeowners want the character of wide boards with better dimensional stability than solid wood provides.

H2: Flooring Questions

**What is the best flooring for a Connecticut home?**

The best flooring depends on the room, the level of the home, and the specific moisture conditions at your property. Solid hardwood works well in above-grade living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms in Connecticut homes that maintain stable indoor humidity. Luxury vinyl plank is the most reliable choice for basements and below-grade spaces, it is fully waterproof and stable through humidity swings. Tile performs well in bathrooms, kitchens, and entryways. Engineered hardwood is appropriate for rooms over concrete slabs, for below-grade spaces with confirmed dry conditions, and in coastal or moisture-prone areas where solid hardwood would move excessively. Carpet remains a practical choice for bedrooms and above-grade recreation rooms where moisture is not a factor.

**How long does flooring installation take?**

Most residential flooring projects in Connecticut complete in two to five days. A single room takes one to two days. Multi-room installations covering 250 to 600 square feet take two to three days. Whole-floor installations covering 600 to 1,200 square feet take three to five days. If you are installing solid hardwood, add three to five days for required on-site acclimation before installation can begin. When flooring is part of a kitchen remodel or basement finishing project, the flooring work is embedded in the overall project schedule.

**Can new flooring be installed over existing flooring?**

In some cases, yes. Luxury vinyl plank and engineered hardwood can often be installed directly over existing hard surfaces provided the subfloor is level, structurally sound, and within acceptable moisture parameters. We assess each situation individually. The key factors are whether the existing surface is level enough, whether added height at thresholds and doors creates problems, and whether the existing material is securely adhered. When overlay installation is not appropriate due to subfloor damage, excessive height buildup, or moisture concerns, we will tell you why removal is the right choice and include it in the proposal.

**What is the difference between solid and engineered hardwood?**

Solid hardwood is milled from a single piece of wood and can be sanded and refinished multiple times over its lifetime. It performs best in above-grade rooms with stable humidity. Engineered hardwood uses a real wood veneer layer over a plywood core. The cross-layer construction makes it significantly more resistant to the expansion and contraction that Connecticut's seasonal humidity changes cause in solid wood. Engineered hardwood is appropriate for below-grade spaces, rooms over concrete slabs, and homes with pronounced seasonal humidity swings. The right choice depends on where the flooring is going and what the moisture conditions are at your home.

**Do I need to move my furniture before flooring installation?**

We move standard furniture within the work area as part of the installation. This includes sofas, chairs, tables, beds, dressers, and similar household furniture. Exceptionally heavy items: grand pianos, large safes, heavy antique armoires, require specialty movers that we can help coordinate in advance. Anything mounted to walls or built into the room should be discussed at the consultation. We will tell you at the proposal stage exactly what we will handle and if anything requires separate coordination.
