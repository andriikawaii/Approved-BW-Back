H2: What Is Included in a Flooring Installation

A complete flooring installation covers subfloor assessment and preparation, removal and disposal of existing flooring, underlayment, the installation itself, transition strips, and base shoe or quarter-round molding, everything from the floor up to finished edges.

Every project begins with a subfloor assessment. We check for level, moisture content, structural integrity, and any soft spots or damage that need attention before new material goes down. In Orange homes, this step is particularly important at the entry level of split-levels, where the slab may be partially below grade. We test slab moisture before recommending any wood product for that area. Above-grade floors are assessed for level, sound structure, and whether existing flooring is worth keeping or removing.

Standard scope includes removal and disposal of existing flooring, subfloor preparation and leveling as needed, appropriate underlayment for the chosen material, the installation to manufacturer specifications, transition strips between rooms and between flooring types, and quarter-round or base shoe molding where the floor meets the baseboard. We move standard furniture within the work area at the start and replace it once the floor is complete.

If we find conditions during installation that change the scope: a larger soft spot than the consultation revealed, a moisture reading above threshold, we stop, communicate, and present options before proceeding.

H2: Flooring Questions

**What is the best flooring for a Connecticut home?**

The right flooring depends on the room, the level of the home, and moisture conditions. For above-grade living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms, solid hardwood is a reliable choice when properly acclimated. For kitchens and high-traffic areas above grade, luxury vinyl plank and tile handle moisture exposure well. For basements and below-grade spaces in Connecticut, LVP is the most reliable option, it is waterproof, stable through humidity swings, and durable under heavy use. Carpet is appropriate for bedrooms and basement rec rooms above grade. In Orange specifically, entry levels on slab call for LVP, not solid hardwood.

**How long does flooring installation take?**

Most residential flooring projects complete in two to five days. A single room takes one to two days. Multi-room installations covering 250 to 600 square feet take two to three days. Whole-floor or whole-home installations take three to five days. Solid hardwood requires an additional three to five days on-site for acclimation before installation begins. When flooring is part of a larger kitchen remodel or basement finishing project, the flooring timeline is built into the overall project schedule.

**Can new flooring be installed over existing flooring?**

In some cases, yes. Luxury vinyl plank and engineered hardwood can often be installed over existing hard surfaces provided the subfloor is level, structurally sound, and within acceptable moisture parameters. The key factors are whether the existing surface is level enough to avoid telegraphing irregularities upward, whether the added height at thresholds and doors creates problems, and whether the existing material is securely adhered. When overlay is appropriate, it saves removal cost and time. When it is not, we tell you why and removal is the correct approach. We do not cover up problems.

**What is the difference between solid and engineered hardwood?**

Solid hardwood is milled from a single piece of wood, typically three-quarters of an inch thick, and can be sanded and refinished multiple times over its lifetime. It performs best in above-grade rooms with stable humidity. Engineered hardwood has a real wood veneer over a plywood core. The cross-layer construction makes it significantly more stable through humidity changes, which is why it works in basements and over radiant heat systems where solid wood would move excessively. In Orange homes with original solid hardwood floors above grade, refinishing that existing solid wood is often the best value. For below-grade or over-radiant applications, engineered hardwood or LVP is the appropriate choice.

**Do I need to move my furniture before flooring installation?**

We move standard furniture within the work area as part of the installation: sofas, chairs, tables, beds, dressers, and similar household furniture. Exceptionally heavy or specialty items such as grand pianos, gun safes, or large anchored furniture require separate coordination, which we can help you arrange in advance. Appliances in kitchen areas are disconnected and reconnected as part of the scope when flooring extends into those spaces. We will confirm at the proposal stage exactly what we will handle and flag anything that needs separate coordination.
