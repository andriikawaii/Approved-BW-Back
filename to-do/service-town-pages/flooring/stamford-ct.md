H2: What Is Included in Flooring Installation

A flooring installation by BuiltWell covers subfloor assessment, removal and disposal of existing flooring, appropriate underlayment, installation to manufacturer specifications, transition strips, base shoe or quarter-round molding, and full cleanup, every step from existing floor out to finished edges.

We assess the subfloor before anything else. In Stamford condos and multi-unit buildings, this means checking the concrete slab for moisture content and ensuring a moisture barrier membrane is installed before any floating floor product goes down. In pre-war buildings with original fir or pine floors, we evaluate thickness, floors that are still three-quarters of an inch or thicker can often be sanded and refinished rather than replaced, which is both more cost-effective and better for the character of the space. Floors that have been sanded multiple times and are now thinner than three-quarters of an inch require replacement instead.

For floating floor installations in Stamford condos and multi-unit buildings, acoustic underlayment is required under all floating floors. This is a building standard in multi-unit construction, and we include it as standard practice, not an upgrade. It reduces sound transmission to the unit below and keeps the installation within what building management requires.

Condo installations in Stamford may require building management approval before work begins. Some buildings require certificate of insurance submissions, elevator scheduling, and noise restriction acknowledgment. We handle this coordination with building management directly so you are not managing logistics between two parties.

H2: Stamford Flooring Conditions We Account For

Stamford's housing stock creates specific installation conditions that affect material selection, preparation requirements, and the logistics of getting the work done.

Concrete subfloors are standard in condos and newer construction throughout Stamford. Before any wood-based or floating floor product is installed over concrete, we test for moisture. Elevated moisture readings mean a moisture barrier membrane is required, this is a non-negotiable step that protects the finished floor and the subfloor warranty. Skipping it to save time or cost is how floors cup, buckle, and fail within the first year.

Pre-war downtown buildings: lofts, apartments, and converted spaces, often have original fir or pine plank floors. If those floors are in sound condition and still at three-quarters of an inch or thicker, refinishing is frequently the better choice. We assess thickness and structural integrity at the consultation and give you a clear recommendation based on what we find, not on which option is more profitable for us.

In multi-unit buildings, acoustic underlayment under floating floors is required, not optional. We include it in every floating floor installation in a Stamford multi-unit building as standard.

Condo and co-op buildings often have their own requirements for contractor work. We have experience coordinating with building management offices, submitting insurance documentation, scheduling elevator access, and working within noise windows. We take this on so the building management relationship does not become your problem to manage.

For single-family homes on Stamford's residential streets and in North Stamford, conditions are more typical of the broader Fairfield County housing stock, colonial and cape homes from the mid-20th century with wood subfloors, some with original hardwood under carpet worth evaluating before replacement.

H2: Flooring Questions

### What is the best flooring for a Connecticut home?

The right flooring depends on the room, the level of the home, and the moisture conditions at your property. Solid hardwood performs well above grade in living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms when properly acclimated. LVP is the most reliable below-grade choice in Connecticut, waterproof, stable through humidity swings, and comfortable underfoot. Tile works well in kitchens, bathrooms, and entryways. For Stamford condos over concrete slabs, LVP and engineered hardwood with a moisture barrier are the appropriate choices. We assess conditions at each home before making a recommendation.

### How long does flooring installation take?

Most flooring projects complete in two to five days. A single room takes one to two days. Multi-room installations covering 250 to 600 square feet typically take two to three days. Whole-floor or whole-home installations take three to five days. Solid hardwood requires an additional three to five days of acclimation on-site before installation begins. Condo projects in Stamford may add a day for building coordination logistics, though we handle that without it affecting the installation schedule.

### Can new flooring be installed over existing flooring?

In some cases, yes. LVP and engineered hardwood can be installed over existing hard surfaces if the subfloor is level, structurally sound, and within acceptable moisture parameters. We assess each situation individually. The key questions are whether the existing surface is level enough, whether the added height creates threshold and door clearance issues, and whether moisture readings are acceptable. When overlay installation is appropriate, it saves removal cost and time. When it is not, we'll tell you directly and explain why.

### What is the difference between solid and engineered hardwood?

Solid hardwood is milled from a single piece of wood and can be sanded and refinished multiple times over its life. It belongs above grade in rooms with stable humidity. Engineered hardwood has a real wood veneer bonded over a plywood core, the cross-layer construction resists the expansion and contraction that Connecticut's humidity swings cause in solid wood. Engineered hardwood is appropriate over concrete slabs, in below-grade spaces, and in homes where seasonal humidity variation is pronounced. In Stamford condos with concrete subfloors, engineered hardwood with a moisture barrier is the appropriate wood-look choice when LVP is not preferred.

### Do I need to move my furniture before flooring installation?

We move standard furniture within the work area as part of the project: sofas, tables, beds, dressers, and similar household items. Exceptionally heavy or specialty pieces, such as grand pianos or large safes, require separate coordination that we can help arrange in advance. In condo buildings, we are careful with common area hallways during furniture moves. We'll confirm exactly what we handle at the proposal stage.
