## What Is Included in a Flooring Installation

A complete flooring installation in New Canaan covers subfloor assessment and preparation, removal and disposal of existing flooring, appropriate underlayment for the selected material, the installation to manufacturer specifications, transition strips between rooms and flooring types, and base shoe or quarter-round molding where the floor meets the baseboard.

We begin every project with a thorough subfloor assessment. In New Canaan, that includes evaluating any existing original flooring before recommending removal. Mid-century modern homes here sometimes feature original terrazzo, concrete, or stone tile that is in sound condition and worth retaining or restoring rather than covering over. Traditional colonials may have original wide-plank hardwood (4-inch to 6-inch profiles) that is architecturally appropriate and a candidate for sanding and refinishing rather than replacement. We assess what is there before we make a recommendation.

For homes with radiant floor heat, which is common in higher-end New Canaan construction, material selection is constrained by what the system can support. Engineered hardwood at 3/8-inch maximum thickness or luxury vinyl plank products rated for radiant applications are the appropriate choices. Solid hardwood and standard carpet are not compatible with radiant systems and we will not specify them for those spaces.

New Canaan homeowners who have renovated before expect to see material samples before work begins and to understand the installation sequence in detail. We provide both.

## Flooring Considerations Specific to New Canaan

New Canaan's architectural character and construction patterns require specific knowledge that affects how flooring work is planned and executed here.

For homes in or near the Historic District, interior flooring changes typically do not require Historic District Commission review. That said, if your project involves any scope that extends to the exterior (a door threshold, an exterior transition, or any work that touches the building envelope), we recommend confirming the scope with the Historic District Commission before work begins. We help clarify what requires review and what does not.

Wide-plank hardwood profiles in 4-inch to 6-inch widths are historically appropriate for New Canaan's traditional colonials. When clients prefer to match an existing original floor in a home addition or adjacent room, sourcing period-correct profiles takes additional lead time. We communicate that clearly during the planning phase so material procurement does not delay the installation schedule.

Radiant heat is present in a significant share of New Canaan's higher-end homes. If your home has a radiant system, tell us at the consultation. The flooring specification depends on it, and selecting the wrong material can damage both the floor and the heating system.

## Flooring Questions

### What is the best flooring for a Connecticut home?

The best flooring depends on the room, the level of the home, and the conditions at your property. For above-grade living rooms and dining rooms in New Canaan colonials, wide-plank solid hardwood in period-correct profiles is often the most appropriate choice architecturally. For kitchens, entryways, and high-traffic areas, luxury vinyl plank or tile handles moisture and daily wear well. For homes with radiant heat, engineered hardwood at 3/8-inch maximum thickness or luxury vinyl plank rated for radiant systems is required. For basements and below-grade spaces, luxury vinyl plank is the reliable choice throughout Connecticut.

### How long does flooring installation take?

Most flooring projects in New Canaan complete in two to five days. A single room takes one to two days. Multi-room installations covering 250 to 600 square feet take two to three days. Full-floor or whole-home installations take three to five days. Projects requiring subfloor repair, concrete leveling, or a restoration assessment on original flooring may extend the timeline. Solid hardwood requires three to five days of on-site acclimation before installation begins, which we schedule before the installation date so it does not create a delay once the crew arrives.

### Can new flooring be installed over existing flooring?

In some situations, yes. Luxury vinyl plank and engineered hardwood can be installed over existing hard surfaces if the subfloor is level, structurally sound, and within acceptable moisture parameters. In New Canaan homes where original terrazzo or stone tile is in good condition, we assess whether overlay is appropriate or whether the original surface should be restored instead. We evaluate each situation individually and explain our recommendation clearly before any decision is made.

### What is the difference between solid and engineered hardwood?

Solid hardwood is milled from a single piece of wood and can be sanded and refinished multiple times over its lifetime. It is the right choice for above-grade rooms with stable humidity and is particularly appropriate in New Canaan's traditional colonial homes where period-correct profiles matter. Engineered hardwood uses a real wood veneer over a plywood core. The cross-layer construction resists the expansion and contraction that Connecticut's humidity swings cause in solid wood, making it the right specification for below-grade spaces, rooms over concrete, and homes with radiant heat. For radiant systems, engineered hardwood must be 3/8-inch thick or less. We recommend the right product for each space based on the actual conditions.

### Do I need to move my furniture before flooring installation?

We move standard furniture within the work area as part of the installation, sofas, chairs, tables, beds, and dressers. Heavy or specialty items such as grand pianos, large antique armoires, or gun safes require specialty movers, which we can help coordinate in advance. Appliances in kitchen areas are disconnected and reconnected as part of the scope when flooring extends there. We confirm exactly what we will handle and what requires separate coordination at the proposal stage so there are no surprises on installation day.
