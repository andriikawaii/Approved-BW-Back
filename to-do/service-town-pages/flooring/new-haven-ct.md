H2: What Is Included in a Flooring Installation

A complete flooring installation covers subfloor assessment and preparation, removal and disposal of existing flooring, underlayment, the installation itself, transition strips, and base shoe or quarter-round molding, from the floor up to finished edges.

In New Haven homes, the subfloor assessment step carries particular weight. Pre-war homes in neighborhoods like Wooster Square, Fair Haven, and portions of East Rock commonly have multiple flooring layers stacked on top of one another: hardwood under linoleum under vinyl, sometimes with a layer of adhesive-backed tile in between. A floor demo assessment determines the actual subfloor condition before material is ordered or installation is scheduled. We do not assume what is there, we confirm it.

Standard scope includes removal and disposal of existing flooring, subfloor preparation and leveling as needed, appropriate underlayment for the chosen material, the installation to manufacturer specifications, transition strips between rooms and between flooring types, and quarter-round or base shoe molding where the floor meets the baseboard. We move standard furniture within the work area at the start and replace it once the floor is complete.

For pre-1978 homes in New Haven, which includes virtually all of Wooster Square, much of East Rock, and the older sections of Westville and Fair Haven, we work under EPA RRP (Renovation, Repair, and Painting) certification protocols when disturbing surfaces that may contain lead paint. This is a legal requirement and a practical safety measure. We handle it as standard practice on qualifying projects.

If we encounter conditions during installation that change the scope: a deeper problem in the subfloor, a moisture reading above threshold, additional layers not visible at the consultation, we stop, communicate, and present options before proceeding.

A professional assessment of remaining board thickness is required before recommending refinishing. In Wooster Square, the 1890s and 1920s rowhouses commonly have wide-plank fir floors and, in pre-1978 homes, potential lead paint in finish layers. Our crews are EPA RRP certified and follow required protocols for lead paint disturbance on qualifying projects. This is not optional for pre-1978 construction where painted surfaces are being disturbed, and we handle it as standard practice.

For Yale-adjacent neighborhoods that include a mix of owner-occupied homes and rental properties, luxury vinyl plank is the most durable and practical flooring choice. It is fully waterproof, holds up to consistent heavy use, and requires minimal maintenance. LVP does not carry the material sensitivity of solid hardwood, making it well-suited for properties that see regular tenant turnover or high occupancy.

Across New Haven's pre-war housing stock, multiple layers of flooring are common. We conduct a floor demo assessment to determine actual subfloor condition before material selection is finalized. What is underneath matters as much as what goes on top.

H2: Flooring Questions

**What is the best flooring for a Connecticut home?**

The right flooring depends on the room, the level of the home, and the specific conditions at your property. For above-grade living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms in New Haven's owner-occupied homes, solid hardwood is a proven choice when properly acclimated, provided the existing floors are not already close to the end of their refinishable life. For kitchens and high-traffic areas above grade, luxury vinyl plank and tile both handle moisture exposure reliably. For basements and below-grade spaces, LVP is the most dependable choice in Connecticut: waterproof, stable through humidity changes, and comfortable underfoot. For rental properties and high-traffic owner-occupied homes, LVP is practical throughout the main living areas. We make the recommendation based on what your home actually requires.

**How long does flooring installation take?**

Most residential flooring projects complete in two to five days. A single room takes one to two days. Multi-room installations covering 250 to 600 square feet take two to three days. Whole-floor or whole-home installations take three to five days. Solid hardwood requires an additional three to five days on-site for acclimation before installation begins. In New Haven homes where a pre-demo floor assessment is needed to evaluate multiple flooring layers, that assessment is scheduled before the installation date so it does not add to the installation timeline. When flooring is part of a kitchen remodel or basement finishing project, the flooring timeline is built into the overall project schedule.

**Can new flooring be installed over existing flooring?**

In some cases, yes. Luxury vinyl plank and engineered hardwood can often be installed over existing hard surfaces provided the subfloor is level, structurally sound, and within acceptable moisture parameters. In New Haven's pre-war homes, however, multiple layers of existing flooring are common, and stacking additional material on top can create height problems at thresholds and doors, and may conceal subfloor damage. We conduct a floor demo assessment to determine whether overlay is appropriate or whether removal is the better path. We do not cover up problems, and we explain the reasoning clearly either way.

**What is the difference between solid and engineered hardwood?**

Solid hardwood is milled from a single piece of wood, typically three-quarters of an inch thick, and can be sanded and refinished multiple times over its lifetime. It performs best in above-grade rooms with stable humidity. In New Haven's older homes, many of the original fir and pine floors are solid wood, and whether they can be refinished depends on how much thickness remains. Engineered hardwood has a real wood veneer over a plywood core. The cross-layer construction makes it significantly more stable through humidity changes, which is why it works below grade and over radiant heat where solid wood would move excessively. We assess the specific conditions at your home and recommend accordingly.

**Do I need to move my furniture before flooring installation?**

We move standard furniture within the work area as part of the installation: sofas, chairs, tables, beds, dressers, and similar household furniture. Exceptionally heavy or specialty items such as grand pianos, gun safes, or large anchored furniture require separate coordination, which we can help you arrange in advance. In New Haven's denser neighborhoods, we also coordinate access and parking for our crew and material deliveries so that logistics do not delay the project start. We will confirm at the proposal stage exactly what we will handle and flag anything that requires separate coordination.
