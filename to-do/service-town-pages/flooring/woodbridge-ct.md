H2: What Is Included in Our Flooring Installation
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A complete flooring installation includes every step from subfloor to finished edges, assessment, removal, preparation, installation, transitions, molding, and cleanup. Here is what that covers in practice.

We begin with a subfloor assessment. We check for level, moisture content, soft spots, and any damage that needs correction before new material goes down. In Woodbridge homes, this step is especially important in basements and on first floors above grade where the surrounding tree canopy and saturated soils can keep moisture elevated through much of the year. We test concrete slabs with a moisture meter before any flooring material is selected for below-grade installation.

Standard installation scope includes removal and disposal of existing flooring, subfloor preparation and leveling where required, appropriate underlayment for the chosen material, the installation itself to manufacturer specifications, transition strips between rooms and flooring types, and quarter-round or base shoe molding at the baseboard. We move standard furniture within the work area at the start and replace it once the floor is complete.

Woodbridge's 1970s and 1980s colonials typically have plywood subfloors in solid condition, a good base for most floating and nail-down installations. Older cape cods in town may have original board subfloors that require a plywood overlay before new material goes down. We assess and communicate this at the proposal stage whenever it is visible, and immediately if we find it once work begins.

We keep job sites quiet and clean. In a town where homeowners value privacy and an undisturbed setting, that matters. We do not run loud equipment before 8am, and we clean up thoroughly at the end of every workday.

H2: Flooring Questions
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

### What is the best flooring for a Connecticut home?

The best flooring depends on the room, the level of the home, and the moisture conditions at your property. For above-grade living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms, solid hardwood is durable and well-suited to Connecticut's climate when properly acclimated. For basements and below-grade spaces, luxury vinyl plank is the most reliable choice, it handles moisture, temperature swings, and daily use without the vulnerabilities of wood or carpet. For kitchens, entryways, and high-traffic areas, LVP and tile both perform well. Carpet remains appropriate for above-grade bedrooms. We assess your specific conditions and recommend accordingly.

### How long does flooring installation take?

Most residential flooring projects complete in two to five days. A single room takes one to two days. Multi-room installations covering 250 to 600 square feet typically take two to three days. Whole-floor installations covering 600 to 1,200 square feet take three to five days. If you're installing solid hardwood, add three to five days for the required acclimation period before installation begins. When flooring is part of a larger renovation, the flooring timeline is built into the overall project schedule and does not extend the overall duration.

### Can new flooring be installed over existing flooring?

In some cases, yes. Luxury vinyl plank and engineered hardwood can often be installed directly over existing hard surfaces: tile, vinyl, or hardwood, when the subfloor is level, structurally sound, and within acceptable moisture ranges. We assess each situation individually. Key factors are whether the existing surface is level enough, whether added height at thresholds and doors creates problems, and whether the existing material is fully adhered. When overlay installation is appropriate, it saves removal time and cost. When it is not, we explain why before proceeding.

### What is the difference between solid and engineered hardwood?

Solid hardwood is milled from a single piece of wood and can be sanded and refinished multiple times over its lifetime. It performs best in above-grade rooms with stable humidity. Engineered hardwood has a real wood veneer bonded over a plywood core, which makes it significantly more resistant to the expansion and contraction caused by humidity changes. Engineered hardwood is appropriate for below-grade spaces, rooms over concrete slabs, and homes where seasonal humidity swings are pronounced. The right choice depends on where the flooring is going and what the moisture conditions are, we'll tell you which one makes sense for each area of your home.

### Do I need to move my furniture before flooring installation?

We move standard furniture within the work area as part of the installation: sofas, chairs, tables, beds, dressers, and similar household items. Heavy or specialty pieces such as grand pianos, large gun safes, or exceptionally heavy antiques require specialty movers, which we can help coordinate in advance. Appliance disconnection and reconnection is included when flooring extends through kitchen areas. Anything wall-mounted or built in should be discussed at the consultation so we can plan accordingly.
