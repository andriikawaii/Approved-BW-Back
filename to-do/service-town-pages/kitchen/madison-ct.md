H2: What Is Included in a Madison Kitchen Remodel
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A full kitchen remodel in Madison covers every trade from demolition through final paint: cabinetry, countertops, tile, flooring, electrical, plumbing, appliance installation, and all required permits and inspections.

We start with demolition and removal of existing cabinets, countertops, and flooring. For coastal Madison properties, the assessment behind the walls is not a formality. Many of the 1940s and 1950s beach cottages in Fence Creek, Middle Beach, and the Hammonasset area were originally built as seasonal structures and converted to year-round use incrementally. That means electrical panels sized for summer use, insulation installed in pieces over decades, and plumbing infrastructure that has been pieced together rather than planned as a system. We assess the actual condition before any finish renovation begins.

Galley kitchens common in Madison's older beach cottages are compact and frequently have load-bearing walls that limit layout options. Opening the floor plan in one of these kitchens requires structural engineering before the finish renovation scope can be finalized. We account for that assessment in the planning phase.

New cabinetry installation includes soft-close hardware and adjustable shelving as standard. For coastal properties, all cabinet hardware is specified at 316 stainless steel rather than standard 304 grade, which corrodes faster in salt air environments. Countertop fabrication and installation, backsplash tile, and underlayment preparation are included. Electrical scope covers new circuits for all major appliances, under-cabinet lighting, and updated outlet and switch locations, including panel assessment for homes that were wired for seasonal use. Plumbing covers all sink, dishwasher, and disposal connections and any relocated water lines. We coordinate appliance delivery and installation.

Interior painting within the kitchen space, drywall patching, and daily cleanup are included throughout the project. The project closes with a final walkthrough.

H2: Coastal and Flood Zone Conditions in Madison
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Madison's coastal kitchen renovations involve flood zone compliance requirements and material specifications that do not apply to inland projects, and most homeowners encounter these requirements after they have already started planning rather than before work begins.

── FEMA Flood Zones and the 50% Rule ────────────────────

Madison has properties in both FEMA Zone VE and Zone AE. The rule that most often affects kitchen renovation scope is the substantial improvement threshold. Under FEMA regulations, if your renovation cost exceeds 50 percent of the pre-improvement market value of the structure (not the land, just the structure), the entire project must comply with current flood zone construction standards. On a modest beach cottage where the structure is assessed at $100,000 and the land carries most of the property value, a $60,000 kitchen renovation crosses that line and triggers full flood compliance requirements for the entire building, including elevation.

We calculate the substantial improvement threshold before writing any proposal for a coastal Madison property. You should know where you stand before scope is discussed, not after a proposal has been built around a number that turns out to be wrong.

── Salt Air Material Specifications ─────────────────────

Properties within 400 to 600 meters of Long Island Sound experience high coastal corrosion rates year-round. Madison's beach neighborhood properties fall within this radius. The practical result is that standard-grade hardware, fasteners, and structural connectors corrode at an accelerated rate in this environment, producing visible failures in 5 to 10 years where properly specified materials hold for 20 to 30.

For coastal Madison kitchens, we specify 316 stainless steel fasteners and hardware throughout, not standard 304 grade. Moisture-resistant flooring, such as luxury vinyl plank or large-format tile, outperforms solid hardwood in high-humidity coastal environments. Appliance selections account for coastal-rated finishes for ranges, refrigerators, and dishwashers. These specifications add 10 to 15 percent to material cost. They also reflect the actual environment the kitchen will live in.

── Galley Kitchen Layouts in Older Cottages ─────────────

The galley kitchens in Madison's 1940s and 1950s beach cottages are compact by design. Load-bearing walls are common along the narrow dimension of these layouts, which limits how the floor plan can be opened without structural work. When a homeowner wants to connect the kitchen to the living or dining area in one of these properties, we assess the structural system before the renovation scope is finalized. Removing or modifying a load-bearing wall requires engineering, beam installation, and a structural permit. That scope affects both cost and timeline and needs to be accounted for from the start, not discovered during demolition.

H2: Permitting and Local Context in Madison
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Kitchen remodeling in Madison requires a building permit from the Madison Building Department, and coastal properties require Coastal Site Plan Review from Planning and Zoning as a separate approval before construction can begin.

The Madison Building Department uses the OpenGov online permitting portal at madisonct.org. Standard building, electrical, and plumbing permits are required for kitchen renovation work involving structural, electrical, or plumbing modifications. We manage all permit applications and inspection scheduling as part of every Madison project.

Coastal properties additionally require Coastal Site Plan Review from Madison's Planning and Zoning Department. This review is a separate approval from the building permit and must be completed before the building permit application can proceed for most exterior work on coastal properties. Homeowners who have not done coastal renovation work before often do not know this review exists until after a project timeline has been built around an assumption that was not accurate. We account for both approval processes in the project schedule from the beginning of the planning phase.

Properties within the Madison Green Historic District or the Liberty Street Historic District require a Certificate of Appropriateness from Madison's Historic District Commission for exterior changes, including changes to windows, doors, siding, and additions. If your property is in either district, we factor HDC review into the project timeline.
