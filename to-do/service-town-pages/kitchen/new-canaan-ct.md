H2: What Is Included in a New Canaan Kitchen Remodel
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A full kitchen remodel in New Canaan covers demolition, cabinetry, countertops, backsplash, flooring, electrical, plumbing, appliance installation, permits, inspections, and daily communication throughout construction.

We begin with demolition and removal of existing cabinets, countertops, and flooring. Before any new material is installed, we assess what is behind the walls. New Canaan homes built between the 1940s and 1970s, including mid-century modern properties, can present conditions that require attention before finish work begins: undersized electrical service, aging plumbing, water intrusion in wall cavities, or structural framing approaches that differ from conventional platform construction. We document what we find, explain what it means for your project, and present options before proceeding.

In mid-century modern homes, the assessment also includes design intent. MCM kitchens were typically designed as part of an integrated first-floor plan, open to adjacent living and dining areas, with material selections that connected to the palette of the exterior and the rest of the interior. A renovation that ignores that intent can undermine the architecture of the home in ways that are difficult and expensive to reverse. We approach MCM renovations with the original design in mind: appropriate cabinetry profiles, material continuity, and sensitivity to sight lines and the relationship between interior and exterior.

Cabinet installation includes soft-close hardware as standard. Countertop fabrication and installation, backsplash tile, and new flooring with proper underlayment are included. Electrical covers new circuits for major appliances, lighting, panel assessment and upgrade when required, and updated outlet and switch locations. Plumbing includes sink and dishwasher connections, garbage disposal installation, and any relocated water lines. We coordinate appliance delivery and installation.

Interior painting within the kitchen, drywall patching, dust barriers throughout construction, and daily cleanup are all part of the project. If the project involves exterior changes that require Historic District Commission review, we handle that coordination as part of the planning process. The project closes with a final walkthrough.

H2: Permitting and Historic Review in New Canaan
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Kitchen remodeling in New Canaan requires separate building, electrical, and plumbing permits, each issued by the town and each requiring its own inspections. Structural changes require stamped plans from a Connecticut-licensed architect or engineer.

The New Canaan Historic District Commission reviews exterior changes to properties within the historic district and the Silvermine neighborhood. If your kitchen renovation includes any exterior modifications (a new window, a door repositioned to the exterior, or changes to the exterior wall envelope), an HDC Certificate of Appropriateness is required before a building permit will be issued. This is a step many contractors do not anticipate, and it can delay a project if it is not identified early.

We manage all permit applications, HDC applications when applicable, and inspection coordination as part of the project. You do not manage any part of the permitting process. Permit review time and any required HDC review windows are built into the project schedule during planning. New Canaan also enforces strict setbacks and floor area ratio limits. If your project involves a kitchen addition or expansion, we verify zoning compliance before scope is finalized.

### Do I need a permit for kitchen remodeling in New Canaan?

Yes. New Canaan requires separate building, electrical, and plumbing permits for kitchen remodeling projects, and each requires its own inspections. Structural changes (removing a wall, adding a beam, modifying a foundation) require plans stamped by a Connecticut-licensed architect or structural engineer before the building permit will be issued. Exterior changes in the historic district or the Silvermine neighborhood require an HDC Certificate of Appropriateness before the building permit is processed. We manage all permit applications and HDC coordination as part of the project. You do not manage any part of that process.

### How much does a kitchen remodel cost in New Canaan?

Kitchen remodeling in New Canaan ranges from approximately $50,000 for a mid-range renovation to $200,000 or more for a full custom or architecturally considered renovation with structural changes. High-end projects fall between $100,000 and $160,000. The Full Custom and MCM tier starts at $160,000. Pre-1970s homes commonly require a panel upgrade from 100 to 200 amps once walls are open. That is a code requirement at that point, not optional, and we include it in the scope when it applies. Design-forward material selections such as custom cabinetry, stone countertops, and integrated appliances are standard in this market and are reflected in the ranges above.

### Can you work with mid-century modern homes in New Canaan?

Yes. MCM kitchen renovation is not the same as a standard kitchen renovation, and treating it as such produces results that work against the architecture. MCM kitchens were designed as part of a continuous open plan, with cabinetry that reads as integrated rather than freestanding, material selections tied to the rest of the home's palette, and sight lines to the exterior that were intentional. We approach these renovations with the original design intent in mind: selecting appropriate cabinetry profiles, maintaining material continuity, and respecting the relationship between the kitchen and the adjacent living spaces. A renovation that enhances the architecture and one that fights it are not the same thing. We understand the difference.

We finalize cabinet selections early in the planning phase and order immediately so lead time runs alongside pre-construction work. HDC review, when required, typically adds two to four weeks to the pre-construction timeline. We build that into the schedule. A mid-range to high-end project from signed proposal to final walkthrough typically runs sixteen to twenty weeks total when you account for cabinet lead time and permitting.
